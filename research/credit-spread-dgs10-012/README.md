# Credit-spread changes lead 10-year Treasury yields (study 012)

Twelfth empirical study in myquant Stage 3, and the fifth real use
of the Stage 6c scaffold. Research question: does the latest available
BAA10Y credit-spread change predict the next-day DGS10 change,
beyond DGS10's own history (including the lag-2 mechanical guard)
and the SPX common-factor control?
This is the planner's rank-1 candidate pair after study 011 consumed
BAA10Y→VXN (6a, total score 1.00, adjacent supported edge E0026).

## Design

The predictor is the daily BAA10Y level change in basis points (not
the log return the planner draft names — a spread quoted in
percentage points has no meaningful log return). The target is the
DGS10 daily **level** change in basis points (not the log return the
draft names either — a yield quoted in percentage points has no
meaningful log return). The expected sign is **negative**:
credit-spread widening reflects credit stress, and the same risk-off
impulse drives flight-to-safety flows into Treasuries, pushing prices
up and yields down. The draft's "positive, out-of-sample component"
refers to positive incremental explanatory power (incremental
R-squared above zero), not to a positive coefficient.

The base model predicts the next-day DGS10 change from DGS10's own
lagged change (lag 1), its own lag-2 change (the mechanical guard —
see below) and the SPX log return; the augmented model adds the
lagged BAA10Y change. The test family has one test (the primary),
Holm-adjusted. Verdict criteria (study 003's three, no cost gate —
DGS10 is a yield and is not directly tradable): Holm-adjusted primary
p below 0.05, primary partial R-squared at least 0.005, holdout
out-of-sample incremental R-squared above 0. The cost gate is
informational only (gross sign-rule edge in basis points per day, no
costs applied, study 003's precedent) and carries no verdict weight.

The full design lives in `design.json`; the registered spec is in
`hypotheses/registrations/H0017/v1.json`.

Sample windows (21:00 UTC daily closes): train [2003-01-02,
2020-01-01), validation [2020-01-02, 2024-01-01), holdout
[2024-01-02, 2026-11-19), each separated by a one-day purge gap.
Minimum observations: 1000 / 500 / 300. The sample is the full
window: DGS10 is still published, so the window runs to the fetch
date.

### Mechanical confound (the core design issue)

BAA10Y is defined as the BAA yield minus DGS10, so the lag-2
predictor contains minus the target's own lag-2 change by
construction: ΔBAA10Y(t−2) = ΔBAA(t−2) − ΔDGS10(t−2). If DGS10
changes are autocorrelated, the predictor could "predict" the target
purely mechanically — and the mechanical sign is negative, the same
as the economic (flight-to-safety) sign. The baseline therefore
controls for the target's own lag-2 change in addition to its lag-1
change. That control is PIT-clean: DGS10 posts on the next business
day (study 007, N=1), so the change ending at t−2 is available at
the decision close. After the guard, the predictor's incremental
information comes essentially from the ΔBAA(t−2) component. The
guard is a mechanical guard, not a factor control: it is retained in
the no-factor upper bound, where "no-factor" means no SPX, not no
guard. The decomposition checks — the predictor-guard correlation,
the lagged-ΔBAA supplementary regression, and the DGS10 change
autocorrelations — quantify the mechanical component and are reported
alongside the primary result. This is the same class of caveat as
E0026's (the BAA10Y cross-section edge, SUPPORTED but partly
mechanical). Whatever the verdict, the result must not be presented
as clean transmission evidence without the caveat.

### Publication lag (the core PIT issue)

BAA10Y posts about a day later than other FRED series (6b evidence
probe 2026-11-18T21:45Z: BAA10Y latest 2026-11-16, DGS10 already
2026-11-17). FRED daily values generally post on the next business day
(study 007, N=1), so BAA10Y's effective availability lag is
**2 trading days**: at the close of day t, the latest available
BAA10Y value is dated t-2, and the latest available change is the
change ending t-2. The predictor for the DGS10 change over t+1
is therefore the BAA10Y change ending at t-2 — a lag-2 predictor by
value date, deliberately. The SPX control carries no lag: Yahoo posts
the close the same evening, so the SPX log change over day t is
knowable at the decision close (study 003's factor-alignment
precedent, SPX[t]). Both lags are applied in the trial's panel
construction via the study-007 lagged-changes module
(`myquant.studies.publication_lag.lagged_changes_on_index`); the PIT
store itself records `available_at` equal to the event time (adapter
limitation — see the design's known confounders). The fetch script
re-verifies the posting pattern behaviorally and records the observed
last dates in the manifest.

One convention to note: the own-history baseline uses the DGS10
change over day t (lag 1) as a predictor for the change over t+1.
Under the study's source-based PIT convention (FRED posts day-t
values the next business day), the day-t DGS10 value is published the
next morning, so the baseline uses information the FRED source had
not yet published at the decision close. The same convention was used
in studies 003, 009, 010 and 011. It strengthens the baseline, so the
incremental test is conservative. The lag-2 guard control is
different: it is PIT-clean (see above), and the two must not be
conflated.

### Researcher decisions beyond the planner draft

1. Predictor is the level change in basis points, not the log
   return (see above). Target is the level change in basis points
   too — a yield has no meaningful log return.
2. The universe adds `index:sp500` as the common-factor control leg.
   The draft's research question assumes a common-factor control but
   names none; the lab convention (studies 001/003/008/010/011) is a
   third-asset control in the primary test. It enters unlagged
   (control_lag_steps=0) because Yahoo posts the close the same
   evening.
3. **V2 amendment**: v1 added `credit:baa-yield` (FRED BAA) as a
   fourth leg for the decomposition check, but the fetch showed FRED
   publishes BAA monthly only (286 rows, 2003-01 to 2026-10), so no
   daily BAA series exists to fetch. V2 drops the BAA leg and derives
   the daily BAA change from the identity ΔBAA = ΔBAA10Y + ΔDGS10.
   Given the lag-2 guard, the lagged-ΔBAA supplementary regression is
   a restatement of the primary test, not independent evidence. The
   amendment was registered before any trial ran (H0017 v2,
   fingerprint `dd5e04a370da48c0185eedb78990dea2164d44f1097db646d5e9764a1e9eed2a`).
4. The baseline controls for the target's own lag-2 change as a
   mechanical guard (see above).
5. The BAA10Y predictor is lagged 2 trading days by availability
   (see above).
6. The cost gate is informational only (DGS10 is a yield and is not
   directly tradable; free proxy series exist — Yahoo ZN=F and IEF
   both return data, verified 2026-11-19 — but converting a
   yield-change signal into a futures or ETF position requires a
   DV01/duration conversion and futures roll handling that this study
   does not model), so the verdict takes three criteria instead of
   the draft's four. Study 003's precedent.

## Results

**Verdict: H0017 v2 REJECTED** (trial T0020, ledger record
`6b87286c14dd70b572665cda0e1dac85b2888f6d3fb9b70d3bd2c290c2b7ad13`).
Primary (n = 5,957, HAC max lag 5): coefficient −0.049085 per bp
(the preregistered negative sign — the first of the five BAA10Y
lead-lag studies whose primary coefficient points the expected way),
SE 0.036773, p = 0.1819, Holm-adjusted p = 0.1819, partial R² =
0.000576 — 8.7x below the preregistered minimum effect size of
0.005 and 2.3x below the MDE. The no-factor upper bound (guard
retained) is the same null (p = 0.2342), and the naive lookahead
alignment is also null (p = 0.1384), so the publication lag is not
what killed the effect. The reverse placebo (DGS10 → BAA10Y) is
nominally significant (p = 0.01941) but descriptive only, below the
0.005 threshold (partial R² = 0.003631), and itself mechanically
confounded. Walk-forward: all-OOS incremental R² −0.000617;
holdout (716 rows) −0.004563. The informational cost assessment
reports a gross sign-rule edge of −0.165bp per day with a 95% CI
that includes zero (bootstrap p = 0.4188, sign accuracy 47.3%) —
not a verdict criterion. Zero of three verdict criteria pass. MDE
(partial R², 80% power) is 0.001316, so the study rules out any
effect at or above ~0.0013. The decomposition: predictor–guard
correlation −0.3869 (the mechanical component is substantial, as
constructed); DGS10 change autocorrelations lag-1 −0.0118, lag-2
−0.0503 (the mechanical channel was small); the lagged-ΔBAA
supplementary regression is numerically identical to the primary
(a restatement, not independent evidence, as the v2 design states).
Guard misaligned rows: 10 of 5,958 (0.17%). Full results,
robustness and limitations: the study report
(`docs/studies/credit-spread-dgs10-012.md`).

## Running

From the repo root, with the dev environment installed:

```bash
.venv/bin/python research/credit-spread-dgs10-012/preregister.py
.venv/bin/python research/credit-spread-dgs10-012/fetch_data.py
.venv/bin/python research/credit-spread-dgs10-012/run_trial.py
```

Preregistration must precede any result-level analysis. Coverage
checks (date ranges, bar counts, missing bars) and the publication-lag
verification are the only pre-trial data contact allowed.
